Case Study 006 · Concept Prototype

Product Design · Brand Identity · Responsible AI

Con Cura

A care companion for orchid growers. The app should make you feel like a good plant parent, not a good app user.

Role
Solo designer: research, concept, brand, UX and UI
Project Type
Self-directed concept prototype
Focus
Care UX, Brand Identity, AI Health Check, Responsible AI
Tools
Figma, Claude
About this project: Con Cura is a concept prototype, not a shipped product. It covers the platform idea, brand identity, a ten-screen clickable prototype, and early usability testing with members of a local orchid society. The AI health check results in the prototype are scripted.
01

The Problem

I grow orchids. For a while I tracked them in a popular orchid care app. It stored the data well. But it was clunky and hard to use, and opening it never felt like a pleasure. So I stopped opening it, and my orchids suffered.

Nothing was broken. The app simply gave me no reason to come back. For a product built around consistent care, that is the failure that matters most.

The app worked. My orchids still suffered.

That made me the user this project is for. I had lived the problem, and I could reach other growers through my local orchid society chapter.

02

From App to Platform

The turning point came when I realized a pet care app would have almost the same structure. Tracking medications, vet visits, and allergies is the same job as tracking water and fertilizer. It is about caring for something you love and wanting to do your best for it.

Steady daily care beats big bursts of attention followed by long gaps.

That is true for orchids, pets, houseplants, and our own health. So Con Cura became one underlying system that changes its subject and accent color depending on what you care for.

App 01

For Orchids

Portfolio, passion, and community access. Where the system is proven.

App 02

For Plants

A broader houseplant audience and the first real revenue.

App 03

For Pets

Mass market and strong word of mouth.

App 04

For You

Personal wellness. The full platform realized.

Starting with orchids is deliberate. I understand the user, the stakes are low enough to do it right, and each app can fund and validate the next.

03

The Guiding Principle

My first instinct was "Duolingo, but for orchids." Not the owl or the streaks. The feeling: you want to come back, progress is visible, and it feels like a relationship instead of data entry.

But gamification tips over fast. I wanted mostly a beautiful companion app with just a little of that pull. The test for every feature: does it serve the plant, or does it only serve the app?

Kept

  • Gentle care streaks framed around the plant
  • Bloom logging as a full-screen memory
  • A quiet health status for each plant
  • A visual care timeline that doubles as a record
  • Warm reminders: "Rosie is probably thirsty"

Left out

  • Points, coins, or any currency
  • Leaderboards or comparing growers
  • Penalties for missed care
  • Mascots or animated characters
  • Level-ups, or "game over" for a lost plant

The app should make you feel like a good plant parent, not a good app user.

04

Naming and Identity

The name had to work for plants, pets, and people. It had to carry warmth without feeling childish. It took the long way.

Petal showed the structure I wanted: one word that travels across subjects. Rootly sparked right away but still read plant-first. Fondly was a real word with real feeling, and I landed on it before the suggestion was finished. A trademark search ruled it out. So I looked at other languages. Cura is Latin for care. I love Italian, and the phrase con cura moved me.

In Italian, con cura is how an artisan signs their work. A letter written with care. A meal prepared with care. I spent years in pastry kitchens, and I know what that kind of attention looks like.

Not care as a concept. Care as a practice.

Type and color

Of three brand directions, the warm editorial one felt finished the moment I saw it. "Cura" is set in Playfair Display Bold, with "CON" as a small Lato eyebrow above it. It reads like a botanical journal, not an app logo.

I narrowed six colors to five and assigned each to an app in a single message, without being prompted. One change came in the prototype: the brief called the orchid color mauve, but on screen it looked washed out. I deepened it to a violet Vanda purple, which reads richer and gives better contrast for text.

05

The Prototype

Ten screens cover one complete care loop: adding a plant, noticing what it needs, logging care, celebrating a bloom, checking its health, and getting a second opinion from a person. Every path returns to the plant's profile, so each one adds to the same story.

06

Designing AI That Knows Its Limits

The health check lets a grower take a photo and get a gentle read on how the plant is doing. Plant identification and diagnosis already exist. What I cared about was how the app delivers the news.

01 · The grower starts it

"Check on her" is a choice, never an automatic scan.

02 · Honest about confidence

A simple meter shows how sure it is, and the language matches: "looks like," not "is."

03 · One thing to do today

What it sees, what that usually means, and a single next step. No wall of possibilities.

04 · No alarm tone

Bad news sounds like a knowledgeable friend, and the screen says plainly: a gentle read, not a diagnosis.

05 · It can say "I can't tell"

When the photo is too dark or the roots are out of frame, it asks for another look instead of guessing, and points to an experienced grower for anything serious.

An AI that admits it doesn't know is one people can learn to trust.

07

Letting the User Decide

Partway through, something felt off. The app was calling a plant "her." Some growers talk about their orchids that way. Others would find it too much.

So I made it the user's choice. When you add an orchid, you pick how the app refers to it: she, he, they, or just its name. The copy adapts everywhere after that. "Check on her" becomes "Check on them" or "Check on Rosie."

It's a small detail, but it reflects the larger idea behind my work: the system should adapt to the person, not the other way around.

09

How AI Was Used

This project was developed with Claude as a working partner, and I want to be clear about the roles. Claude was the research tool, the naming sounding board, and a fast way to see options. I was the designer.

Naming

Suggestions, then my call

Claude suggested Rootly; I said it wasn't quite there. Claude offered related names; I landed on Fondly before it finished the list.

Brand

Options, then my eye

Claude showed three brand directions and six colors. I chose the editorial direction immediately, kept five colors, and knew which app each belonged to.

Screens

A first pass, then my changes

Claude built a first pass of the screens in Figma from my brand brief. I made the calls: a deeper orchid purple, the she, he, they choice, and the second opinion path.

Judgment

The skill underneath

Knowing when to take a suggestion, when to push back, and when the answer is already in your head is what separates designing with AI from letting it design for you.

10

What's Next

If a product exists to help people care, it has to be built with care too.
